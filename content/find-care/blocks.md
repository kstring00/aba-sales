## Where to search: under 3

For children under 3, start with your state's early intervention program. In Texas, that's Early Childhood Intervention (ECI), run by Texas Health and Human Services for children from birth to 36 months.

- **You can contact ECI yourself.** A referral doesn't have to come from a doctor, and your child doesn't need an autism evaluation first.
- **The ECI evaluation is free.** ECI may ask permission to bill your insurance. Ask your local program about any cost for ongoing services.
- **Services usually happen at home** or in other everyday places.

You can ask your pediatrician about an autism evaluation at the same time. You don't have to wait for one before starting the other.

## Where to search: school-age

From age 3, public school districts must find and evaluate children who may have a disability, at no cost to families, even if your child doesn't attend that school yet. This is called Child Find, and it comes from a federal law, IDEA.

- **Ask in writing** and keep a copy. In Texas, the district has 15 school days to respond to a written request, and usually 45 school days to finish the evaluation once you give consent.
- **If your child qualifies**, the school writes an IEP at a meeting Texas calls an ARD.
- **School services are separate** from therapies through insurance, such as ABA, speech or OT. Many children have both.

SPEDTex, the Texas special education information center, answers parents' questions at 1-855-773-3839.

## Where to search: Medicaid or CHIP

- **Use your health plan's directory.** Most children with Medicaid or CHIP in Texas are in a managed care plan, such as STAR, STAR Kids or CHIP. Call the member services number on the card, or search the plan's online directory, for in-network evaluations, ABA, speech and OT.
- **Texas Medicaid covers ABA** for members 20 and under with autism, since February 2022. It needs prior authorization, and the provider usually handles that.
- **Ask for a service coordinator.** Many plans have them, and they can help you find providers with openings.

If your child isn't covered yet, you can apply at Your Texas Benefits or by calling 2-1-1.

## Where to search: private insurance

Use your insurer's online provider directory, or call the member services number on the card, and ask for in-network providers for the service you need. Questions worth asking on that call:

1. Is ABA (or speech therapy, or OT) covered under my plan?
2. Do we need a referral or an evaluation first, and from whom?
3. Is prior authorization required, and who submits it?
4. What will we pay: deductible, copay or coinsurance? Is there a limit on visits or hours?
5. Is my plan fully insured or self-funded?

That last question matters. Texas's autism coverage law applies to state-regulated, fully insured plans. Many large employers' self-funded plans follow federal rules instead, so ask your HR department for the plan's summary of benefits. For every call, write down the date, the name of the person you spoke with, and a reference number.

## Where to search: no coverage or not sure

- **Check Medicaid and CHIP.** Income limits for children are higher than many parents expect. In Texas, apply at Your Texas Benefits or by calling 2-1-1.
- **Free evaluations don't depend on insurance.** ECI evaluates children under 3, and school districts evaluate children 3 and up, at no cost.
- **Ask 2-1-1 about local help**, including sliding-scale clinics, family support and the state-funded Texas Children's Autism Program for ages 3 to 15.
- **University clinics and training programs** sometimes offer lower-cost evaluations or services, often with waitlists.

If you have insurance but aren't sure what it covers, call the number on the card and ask the questions listed for private insurance.

## Outside Texas

The details on this page are for Texas. Every state has its own versions, often with different names:

- an early intervention program for children under 3
- Child Find evaluations through the public school district from age 3
- Medicaid and CHIP, with their own rules for autism services

Search "[your state] early intervention" and "[your state] Medicaid autism services", or call 2-1-1 for local help. The questions, call script and red flags below apply anywhere.

## Questions to ask a provider

1. Are you in network with our insurance, and do you handle prior authorization?
2. How long is your waitlist, and can you add us today?
3. Who would work with my child, and what are their credentials? How often does the supervising BCBA or licensed therapist see my child?
4. What does the first assessment involve, and how long does it take?
5. How do you set goals with families?
6. How will I be involved, and can I watch sessions?
7. What do you do when a child is upset or wants to stop?
8. What hours and locations do you offer?

## A short call script

"Hi, my name is ___. I'm calling about services for my child, who is ___ years old. We're looking for ___ [an autism evaluation, ABA, speech therapy]. Our coverage is ___. Are you taking new clients, and do you accept our coverage? If there's a waitlist, can we be added today, and about how long is it?"

Before you hang up, write down who you spoke to, the date, and the next step.

## Red flags

General signs to slow down and ask more questions:

- promises of a specific result, or claims that a child will "recover" or become "indistinguishable" from other children
- being discouraged from watching sessions or asking questions
- vague answers about who supervises, or credentials you can't confirm (you can check any BCBA or RBT on the BACB's public registry)
- goals centered on stopping harmless behaviors, like stimming, or on looking "normal"
- punishment, restraint or seclusion outside a true emergency, or vague answers about when they're used
- pressure to sign up fast, pay a lot upfront, or commit to many hours before an assessment
- your concerns, or your child's distress, being brushed aside

## What a waitlist usually involves

Waitlists for evaluations and therapy are common, and they can last months. It isn't a sign you did anything wrong.

- **Join several at once.** It's normal, and providers expect it.
- **Ask each one** how long the wait usually is and whether they keep a cancellation list.
- **Check in every few weeks** with a short, friendly call.
- **Start what you can.** ECI (under 3) and the school district (3 and up) can often begin sooner, and notes from the ABC tracker are useful to bring to intake.
