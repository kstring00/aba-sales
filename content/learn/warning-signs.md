## What parents often notice

Looking back, many parents find that a hard moment rarely came from nowhere. There were small changes first. Every child is different, but parents often describe things like:

- covering ears or eyes, or moving away from noise or light
- pacing, or moving much more, or much less, than usual
- getting quiet, or getting louder
- repeating a word, phrase or question
- a change in breathing, face color or muscle tension
- saying "no" to things they usually enjoy
- clinging, or pushing people away

Some of these are also everyday ways a child copes or has fun. One sign on its own doesn't mean a hard moment is coming. What matters is what you learn about your own child over time.

## Writing them down

Early signs are easy to forget once a hard moment is over. The ABC tracker has an optional question, "Did you notice anything before it got big?", with common signs to tap and room for your own words.

After a week or two, the tracker shows which early signs you recorded most often. That's a count, not an explanation, but it gives you and your child's team something concrete to talk about.
